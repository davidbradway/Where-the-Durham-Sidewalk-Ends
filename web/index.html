Where the Durham Sidewalk Ends

How much street frontage in the City of Durham has no sidewalk, and which organizations own the most of it? Most gaps front single-family homes. On busy arterial streets, though, a small group of large owners accounts for a big share of the gap, so they are the natural place to start.

How this was measured

Method
  • Frontage. Each parcel boundary inside city limits is cut into 10 ft pieces. A piece counts as street frontage when it is not shared with another parcel (so it borders the public right-of-way) and lies within 75 ft of a public street centerline. Interstates, ramps, alleys and private drives are excluded.
  • Sidewalk. A frontage piece is covered when a sidewalk from the city's inventory lies within 25 ft. Raising this to 35 ft changes coverage by about one percentage point.
  • Busy streets are minor and principal arterials in the city's street functional class. Collectors, which are mostly two-lane neighborhood streets, count as local.
  • Owners. Names are normalized (punctuation and LLC/INC suffixes removed) and known variants are merged, e.g. Duke University with Duke University Health System, and the several spellings of Durham Public Schools and NCDOT.
Caveats
  • Streets in new subdivisions that the city has not yet accepted are left out. Their sidewalks are usually required but not yet in the inventory.
  • Some frontage is not a realistic sidewalk location: railroad corridors, steep creek banks, and frontage along limited-access roads that are classified as arterials.
  • The parcel layer and the sidewalk inventory don't always line up, so individual parcels can be wrong. Treat owner totals as estimates and check specific sites before outreach.
  • Only organizations are named. Parcels owned by individuals are shown on the map without a name.
